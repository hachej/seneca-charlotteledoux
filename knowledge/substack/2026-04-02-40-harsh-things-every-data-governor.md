## On your role & identity

- Nobody fully understands [what you do](https://thedatagovernanceplaybook.substack.com/p/being-the-first-head-of-data-governance). Get used to explaining it over and over and over again.
- You are not the data police. The moment people see you as the one who says “no”, you’ve already lost. You’re an enabler, not a blocker.
- Your job title doesn’t give you authority. Authority in Data Governance is earned through trust, not through a title on a slide deck.
- You’ll spend at least 80% of your time on change management .
- You are replaceable if you don’t [show value fast](https://thedatagovernanceplaybook.substack.com/p/real-life-wins-in-data-governance). Governance budgets are the first ones cut.
- Being right is not enough. You can have the perfect framework, if people don’t follow it, it’s worth nothing.
- You will feel lonely. Very few people in your company truly get what you’re going through. Find your tribe outside - communities, newsletters, conferences.
- Imposter syndrome is normal. Everyone else is figuring it out too.
---

## On the organization

- If your CEO can’t explain why Data Governance exists, you have a problem.
- Data Governance without a [business case](https://thedatagovernanceplaybook.substack.com/p/from-business-strategy-to-data-governance) is a hobby. Connect every single initiative to revenue, risk reduction, or regulatory compliance .
- The org chart is not your friend. Data doesn’t respect departments. Your biggest challenge is making [cross-functional collaboration](https://thedatagovernanceplaybook.substack.com/p/the-data-domains-map-enigma) happen in a siloed world.
- A reorganization will destroy months of your work. Build your governance to survive org changes.
- Your company already does Data Governance - they just don’t call it that. Find those pockets.
- “We’ll do governance later” means “never.” If it’s not in the project plan from day one, it won’t happen.
- Compliance will get you budget. Business value will keep you alive . Use GDPR and regulations to open the door, but don’t make it your only argument.
- The IT department is not your enemy. Be useful to them.
---

## On people & politics

- [Data Owners](https://thedatagovernanceplaybook.substack.com/p/the-big-confusion-in-data-governance) who didn’t ask for the role won’t act like owners.
- Executives will say “yes” in meetings and do nothing after. That’s not buy-in. Real support means they talk about governance when you’re not in the room.
- Middle management is where governance goes to die. Support and motivate them or lose the game.
- People don’t resist governance. They resist extra work. Frame governance as simplifying their life , not adding to their plate.
- Your biggest ally is someone you haven’t met yet. There’s always a frustrated business analyst, a passionate data engineer, or a quality-obsessed product manager out there. Find them.
- You will be blamed for things that aren’t your fault. The trick is to not take it personally - and use those moments to show what governance actually does.
- Nobody reads your documentation. [Make things visual](https://thedatagovernanceplaybook.substack.com/p/onboarding-your-data-stewards), short, and findable in 30 seconds or less.
- One loud skeptic can undo months of progress. Identify your critics early. Engage them privately.
---

## On tools & frameworks

- A data catalog won’t save you. I’ve seen lots of [catalogs ending as empty shells](https://thedatagovernanceplaybook.substack.com/p/why-data-catalogs-die-and-how-to).
- You don’t need a tool to start. A Notion table, a shared Excel file, a Confluence page. [Start documenting, start standardizing](https://thedatagovernanceplaybook.substack.com/p/data-governance-templates-advanced).
- Your framework will change. Multiple times. The operating model you design in year one will look completely different in year three. That’s fine.
- Perfection is the enemy of governance . Ship something good enough and iterate.
- AI won’t replace your governance work. But it will change it. AI needs governed data to work properly - which makes your job more important, not less.
- Metadata management is boring but essential. It’s the plumbing of your data house.
- Your data quality issues are symptoms, not root causes. Don’t just fix the data. [Fix the process and the ownership gap](https://thedatagovernanceplaybook.substack.com/p/how-to-turn-data-quality-into-business) that created the issue in the first place.
- Dashboards about governance maturity impress executives but change nothing. What changes things is one conversation with a frustrated business user who finally gets the support they needed.
---

## On the long game

- Data Governance is a marathon, not a sprint.
- Quick wins are not optional. They’re your survival strategy.
- You will want to quit. The bureaucracy, the politics, the blank stares in meetings... it gets to you. Take a break. Come back.
- Celebrating small victories is mandatory.
- Your successor needs to be able to continue without you. If governance collapses when you leave, you didn’t build governance. You built a dependency.
- The companies that succeed at governance are the ones that make it boring. Just... [embedded in how things work](https://thedatagovernanceplaybook.substack.com/p/the-governance-by-design-starter).
- Comparing yourself to other companies is dangerous. Their context isn’t yours. Their maturity isn’t yours.
- Despite all of this - the job is worth it. When you see a team finally agreeing on a definition. When a CEO says “let’s check with governance before we decide.” That’s when you know : you’re building something that matters. 💪
