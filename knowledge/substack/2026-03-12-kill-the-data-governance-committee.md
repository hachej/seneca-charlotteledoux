## Why committees fail

Every time I work for a company on Data Governance, they ask me about “comitology”. What a fancy consultant word …

Here's what I’ve seen many times :

12 people in a room (or on Teams, let's be honest) on a monthly basis. A mix of IT leads, only one Business representative , the Chief Data Officer, the whole Data Governance team. One or two hours, lots of slides prepared in a crazy rush like a bomb is about to explode.

## And then during the meeting… everybody is faking progress

Because the goal is now to document 100% of assets, get 90% coverage of owners and stewards, or have 80% data quality score on top KPIs.

I’m not saying these metrics are not good. I’m saying they have become the target :

This is in general at this moment that people start to fake progress. They’ll manage to share that their KPIs have improved, with a nice sun emoji on the slide.

At the end, the committee is not taking decisions . It is barely tracking the evolution and progress of your data governance roadmap, with poor reflection of reality.

This kind of follow-up on your KPIs and roadmap should be done internally with your boss, in general the Chief Data Officer, but not in a Data Governance Committee.

> 🔍 The biggest problem with having a Data Governance Committee is that you’re creating a new silo . You’re treating Data Governance like an isolated topic , when it’s just not.

## What to build instead

You should aim for better coordination inside each domain : they don’t need you, they don’t need a new meeting.

They only need to take new habits within existing meetings :

- In project framing reviews : make ownership a mandatory checkbox, not an afterthought.
- In product specification : data stewards give key definitions, business rules and formula to ensure the development match reality. treat data contracts (who produces what, in what format, by when) as a deliverable alongside features.
- In team weekly meetings : surface data quality issues and schema changes as they happen, and coordinate remediation process.
## Other cool ideas…

📋 A living internal wiki on a Notion/Confluence space where domain teams document their data contracts, quality issues, key decisions and roadmap.

The difference : it’s searchable, async, and owned by the teams, not curated by a central body.

👥 A data champions network with a Teams channel to discuss, and an informal monthly meeting with peers to share what’s working, flag what’s broken, and carry best practices back to their team. Each “data champion” is embedded in a domain, it can be an engineer, analyst, or business contributor.

## When a committee actually makes sense

Ok, I’ll admit it, the official Data Governance committee could be useful in some specific cases.

1️⃣ First case is when you’re looking for buy-in at the very beginning of your Data Governance program . You need to establish legitimacy.

Use it as a launchpad : get the right stakeholders in the room, assign domain owners, and set the key principles.

Example : your CDO convenes a founding committee for 6 months to define what "data ownership" means in your company, which domains exist, and who is accountable. Once that foundation is set, the committee dissolves into the decentralized model : its job is done.

2️⃣ You really act as a central body sharing new standards for all domains, or specific regulation impacting the whole company .

In this case, you have a form of authority, and you act as facilitator by helping transversal decisions between domains.

👉 You can call it “ Cross-domains Review ”.

Example : two domains are using conflicting definitions of "active user" : one counts logins in the last 30 days, the other in the last 7. The cross-domain review brings both teams together, aligns on a single definition, and ensures it's documented and enforced in the data catalog.

The key distinction : this body meets when there is a real cross-domain problem to solve but not on a fixed monthly cadence regardless of whether there's anything to discuss.
