## Why it’s dying

I was surprised by the number of reactions I got when I first mentioned the “dead catalog” trend. It’s sad - but yes, many organizations invest a lot of money on these tools and end up with an empty shell.

## Top 3 reasons I’ve seen

1️⃣ The number one reason is that the content is crappy . The definitions are not completed, not validated and most of all NOT TRUSTWORTHY !

It reminds me when we all opened ChatGPT for the first time a few years ago, and realized after a few prompts that we can’t really trust it. We closed the tab and moved on. (But we came back on it thanks to major improvements 😅)

2️⃣ The second reason that I see often is that it’s hard to actually find what you look for . Which is ironic given that the value proposition of these tools is to help with the discovery…

3️⃣ The third reason is that the data catalog is there, but beside the rest : beside projects, beside workflows, beside data pipelines, and so on. Instead of being deeply embedded in the data stack, which makes it feel like a separate tool rather than a core part of how teams actually build, operate, and govern data.

## Flip the coin

It’s here, it’s a mess. But there’s still hope.

Because if it’s still there, it means that some people (at least the one paying for it - probably IT) are still believing in its value.

Let’s come back a moment on the value proposition . What was it? You must be able to say it in one sentence :

The Data Catalog is the single source of truth for trusted , governed , and reusable data assets accelerating analytics, BI, and AI initiatives by design.

👉 Now, you have to restart your work with a major pain :

What’s the latest complain you’ve heard about understanding data? Is there a big project delivering reports with lots of indicators? Is there a department spending hours right now on debates about what “revenue” actually mean?

🐥 That’s your starting point.

- Tie your data cataloguing work to an ongoing project and explain the value you’ll bring.
“ Ensure every data asset is clearly defined , owned, and contextualized so that all teams understand better the new KPIs of report X and Z”

- Start on a small scope, within one business department .
Convince first the Data Owner of the scope.

- Do a workshop with Data Stewards to review and propose .
For the workshop, you bring the existing content, you show that some work was done but need to be improved. And please, STOP using slides. Instead, have the stewards use the data catalog during the workshop and propose / update definitions.

- Make the Owner and Stewards validate in the data catalog .
They’ll search for the indicator, review and validate in the tool - giving precious feedback about it.

> They can’t find an indicator in less than 30 seconds? That’s a red flag regarding the structure of your catalog !

## Maintain the adoption

Not as easy as it seems. The golden rule is :

> If the catalog doesn’t save people time this week , they won’t use it next week.

The key is to get all teams to use it on a daily basis , where people do their work :

- For Data Teams : make documentation a build requirement of their projects (dataset registered, owned, description validated), link catalog entries in tickets and data incident reports
🤯 A nice idea : integrate it into developer tools. Best integrations are : dbt auto-sync descriptions + tests, IDE/browser extensions, Airflow/Dagster links to asset pages.

- For Business Teams : link catalog entries from BI tools and dashboards, and setup a Catalog chatbot on their Teams channel
- For all employees : give them access as readers during their corporate onboarding, that’s literally the best way for people to use it frequently !
Another way is to “ incentivize contributions ”.

For this one, you have to start with the Data Stewards. Their managers will setup clear performance goals about their stewardship tasks, hence recognizing the work at the end of the year.

Finally, setup a certification approach for your data assets . The “Gold” level will be as followed :

- Fully documented
- Clear Owner and Stewards
- Monitored with quality checks
- Approved for executive reporting
What happens is that teams want their data to reach Gold status because :

- Execs used it
- It is visible
- It is respected
Certification becomes a prestige marker - and suddenly people look and use your data catalog !!
